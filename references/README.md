# Initial bibliography and reading map

This is a starting bibliography, not a complete or neutral review. The literature includes competing interpretations; read primary papers and critiques before adopting any proposed principle.

## Planetary energy budget

- NASA Earth Observatory. “Climate and Earth's Energy Budget.” https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/climate-and-earths-energy-budget/  
  Accessible description of the global-mean incoming solar flux, reflection, absorption, surface-atmosphere exchanges and outgoing thermal radiation.

- Trenberth, K. E., Fasullo, J. T., & Kiehl, J. (2009). “Earth's Global Energy Budget.” *Bulletin of the American Meteorological Society*, 90(3), 311–323. https://doi.org/10.1175/2008BAMS2634.1  
  Review and update of Earth's global annual-mean energy budget, including TOA radiation and energy transport/storage.

## Radiation entropy

- Wu, W., & Liu, Y. (2010). “Radiation entropy flux and entropy production of the Earth system.” *Reviews of Geophysics*, 48, RG4006. https://doi.org/10.1029/2008RG000275  
  Essential for this project. Reviews methods for calculating radiative entropy flux and shows that simple energy-flux/temperature approximations can be substantially inaccurate. It also highlights the importance of whether reflected sunlight is treated as specular or Lambertian.

## Exergy of solar radiation

- Rodríguez, E., Cardemil, J. M., Starke, A. R., & Escobar, R. (2022). “Modelling the Exergy of Solar Radiation: A Review.” *Energies*, 15(4), 1477. https://doi.org/10.3390/en15041477  
  Reviews competing formulations for solar-radiation exergy, including the Petela expression and the assumptions behind radiative exergy models.

## Non-equilibrium thermodynamics and dissipative structures

- Prigogine, I. (1978). “Time, structure, and fluctuations.” *Science*, 201(4358), 777–785. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.201.4358.777  
  Foundational discussion of non-equilibrium and dissipative structures. It does not establish that life or intelligence must emerge.

## Thermodynamics and origin-of-life proposals

- Michaelian, K. (2022). “Non-Equilibrium Thermodynamic Foundations of the Origin of Life.” *Foundations*, 2(1), 308–337. https://doi.org/10.3390/foundations2010022  
  A review from the thermodynamic dissipation theory perspective. Treat it as one particular proposal, not community consensus; compare its assumptions with alternative origin-of-life frameworks.

## Entropy production and ecosystems: tests and limitations

- Meysman, F. J. R., & Bruers, S. (2010). “Ecosystem functioning and maximum entropy production: a quantitative test of hypotheses.” *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B*, 365, 1405–1416. https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2009.0300  
  Tests several entropy-production hypotheses in food-web models. The stronger state-selection and gradient-response claims do not hold generally in the tested model set.

- Chapman, E. J., Childers, D. L., & Vallino, J. J. (2016). “How the Second Law of Thermodynamics Has Informed Ecosystem Ecology through Its History.” *BioScience*, 66(1), 27–39. https://doi.org/10.1093/biosci/biv166  
  Historical review of maximum power and maximum entropy production approaches; notes the lack of widely agreed theoretical derivations for these proposed principles.

## Physical constants

- NIST. “CODATA Value: Stefan–Boltzmann constant.” https://physics.nist.gov/cgi-bin/cuu/Value?sigma  
  CODATA recommended value: 5.670374419… × 10⁻⁸ W m⁻² K⁻⁴.

## Reading priorities

1. Establish standard thermodynamic definitions and balances before reading goal-oriented interpretations.
2. Read Wu and Liu before choosing an entropy-flux approximation.
3. Compare different origin-of-life frameworks; do not read only thermodynamic-dissipation proposals.
4. Distinguish entropy-production constraints from claims that a system maximizes entropy production.
5. Add primary literature on exergy of radiation, autocatalytic networks, prebiotic chemistry and replication when model choices are narrowed.


## Local chemical disequilibrium and prebiotic environments

- IUPAC. “Chemical potential.” *Compendium of Chemical Terminology (Gold Book)*, 5th ed., 2025. https://doi.org/10.1351/goldbook.C01032  
  Defines chemical potential as partial molar Gibbs energy.

- Ianeselli, A. et al. (2023). “Physical non-equilibria for prebiotic nucleic acid chemistry.” *Nature Reviews Physics*, 5, 185–195. https://doi.org/10.1038/s42254-022-00550-3  
  Reviews possible geochemical settings in which temperature, salt, pH, UV and other physical non-equilibria can affect prebiotic nucleic-acid chemistry.

- Barge, L. M., & Price, R. E. (2022). “Diverse geochemical conditions for prebiotic chemistry in shallow-sea alkaline hydrothermal vents.” *Nature Geoscience*, 15, 976–981. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41561-022-01067-1  
  Perspective arguing that shallow alkaline hydrothermal systems merit consideration because of variable chemistry, temperature and wet–dry cycles.

- Rodriguez, L. E. et al. (2024). “A Geological and Chemical Context for the Origins of Life on Early Earth.” *Astrobiology*, 24(1 Suppl.). https://doi.org/10.1089/ast.2021.0139  
  Review of Hadean/Eoarchean geology, geochemical cycles and candidate abiogenesis environments.

- Jackson, J. B. (2016). “Natural pH Gradients in Hydrothermal Alkali Vents Were Unlikely to Have Played a Role in the Origin of Life.” *Journal of Molecular Evolution*, 83, 1–11. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00239-016-9756-6  
  Critical review questioning the evidence for natural pH gradients across suitable inorganic membranes and the plausibility of prebiotic gradient-harvesting machinery.

- Tutolo, B. M. (2026). “Rethinking the origin of life at seafloor hydrothermal vents.” *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 123(36), e2613108123. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2613108123  
  Recent reassessment arguing that ancient serpentinizing systems may differ substantially from modern Lost City analogues, including the magnitude and location of relevant pH gradients.

- Moldogazieva, N. T. et al. (2026). “Redox chemistry of early Earth and the origin of life.” *Communications Chemistry*, 9, 143. https://doi.org/10.1038/s42004-026-01969-w  
  Recent review of competing early-Earth redox scenarios and several candidate local environments.

- Krissansen-Totton, J., Bergsman, D. S., & Catling, D. C. (2016). “On Detecting Biospheres from Chemical Thermodynamic Disequilibrium in Planetary Atmospheres.” *Astrobiology*, 16(1), 39–67. https://doi.org/10.1089/ast.2015.1327  
  Quantifies chemical disequilibrium in planetary atmospheres and demonstrates why aqueous phases and multiphase chemistry can materially change the result.
